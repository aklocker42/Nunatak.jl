struct DIVAShear

Vertical-shear component of the depth-integrated viscous approximation (Goldberg 2011; Lipscomb et al. 2019): the viscosity includes the vertical shear driven by the basal stress, the depth-averaged velocity feels the drag β / (1 + β F₂), and the velocity profile is reconstructed from the shear integral after each solve.

Nunatak.EnthalpyThermodynamics — Type
struct EnthalpyThermodynamics{P, G, TS, TO, SC, ST, LT}

Enthalpy thermodynamics after Aschwanden et al. (2012): one prognostic enthalpy E per layer of the σ grid covers cold ice (E < Eₚₘ) and temperate ice with water fraction ω. Basal melt water is stored in a local layer Hw without transport, and refreezes when the column draws heat from the base. Water above drainage_water_fraction drains from temperate layers into Hw. A temperate base under a temperate layer is insulating (Kleiner et al., 2015, Eq. 6), so all basal heat melts ice there. With grounded_melt_coupling the change of Hw is taken from the ice thickness, so ice and water mass is conserved. Horizontal advection is explicit and first-order upwind, so an energy step needs u Δt / Δx ≤ 1. Stepping the energy on a sparse schedule counts the elapsed time in that limit.

  • parameters::Any: EnthalpyParameters

  • geothermal_flux::Any: geothermal heat flux into the base G (W m⁻²)

  • surface_temperature::Any: surface temperature Tₛ (K), capped at the melting temperature

  • ocean_temperature::Any: temperature of the ocean at the base of floating ice (K)

  • schedule::Any: schedule of the energy steps; the elapsed time since the last step is the time step

  • state::Any: fields and column solver, built by IceSheetModel

  • last_step_time::Any: time of the last energy step

  • grounded_melt_coupling::Bool: whether grounded melt and drainage thin the ice

source

    grid;
    Nσ,

    clock,
    constants,

Build an IceSheetModel on the horizontal grid (z must be Flat). With a rheology the velocities are solved by a StressBalanceSolver in update_state!; pass velocity_solver = nothing to keep them fixed. The thickness is transported by the depth-averaged velocity with advection, fifth-order WENO limited to 0 ≤ H ≤ 100 km by default, which needs a horizontal halo of at least 4; UpwindBiased(order = 1) works with any halo. Cells thinner than masking.minimum_thickness are ice free and drop out of the stress balance; see SmoothIceMask. masking = PartialCellFront(grid) instead advances calving fronts with partially filled cells.

Nunatak.PartialCellFront — Type
struct PartialCellFront{F}

Calving-front advance with partially filled front cells (Albrecht et al., 2011, The Cryosphere 5, 35–44), as in MITgcm STREAMICE. The ice mask μ is a persistent 0/1 flag of full cells, and only full cells enter the stress balance. A cell that is not full but holds ice is a partial front cell: ice of thickness Hʳ (geometry.front_thickness) covers the fraction H / Hʳ of it. It receives thickness from full neighbors flowing into it and passes none on. At the end of each time step Hʳ becomes the mean thickness of the full cells flowing in, or is kept when none does; a cell holding ice without Hʳ takes the mean thickness of its full neighbors, or its own thickness when it has none. A partial cell whose mean thickness reaches Hʳ becomes full with H = Hʳ and splits the excess volume equally among its empty neighbors; this repeats up to passes times. Excess left after the last pass stays in the receiving cells, so the transport conserves volume. Full cells that drain to H = 0 become empty. Surface mass balance and basal melt act on full cells during the step and then, as in STREAMICE, on the ice of the partial cells and of the cells that filled, which empty when the melt removes all of it.

  • overflow::Any: volume O an overfilled front cell passes to each empty neighbor (m³)

  • passes::Int64: maximum number of overflow passes per time step

Nunatak.ShallowIce — Type
struct ShallowIce

Shallow-ice approximation (SIA): the velocity follows locally from the surface slope with no stress-balance solve,

u(ζ) = uᵇ - 2 (ρᵢ g)ⁿ Hⁿ⁺¹ |∇s|ⁿ⁻¹ ∂s/∂x ∫₀^ζ A (1 - ζ′)ⁿ dζ′,

evaluated at the velocity points with the face-averaged thickness and rate factor, the slope across the face averaged from the four neighboring differences. With WeertmanFriction the sliding velocity uᵇ balances the driving stress, C |uᵇ|ᵐ = ρᵢ g H |∇s|; without friction, and at faces where C = 0, the bed is frozen. Floating ice is held still through the grounded fractions φᵘ, φᵛ. The default thickness advection is then Centered(order = 2), so the thickness flux is the diffusive flux -D ∇s of Mahaffy (1976).

julia> model = IceSheetModel(grid; stress_balance = ShallowIce(), rheology = GlenFlowLaw(grid; rate_factor = 1e-24));

julia> summary(model.velocity_solver)
"ShallowIceSolver(schedule = IterationInterval(1))"

struct SmoothIceMask{FT}

Ice mask μ(H) that rises from 0 at minimum_thickness to 1 at minimum_thickness + transition_thickness as a cubic smoothstep, so it is continuously differentiable in H. Nunatak.StressBalanceMultigridPreconditioner — Type
struct StressBalanceMultigridPreconditioner{L, W}

Geometric multigrid V-cycle preconditioner for ssa_operator!. Each level halves Nx and Ny and rediscretizes the operator from the area-weighted average of η̄H and the restricted drag; η̄Hᶠᶠ is interpolated to the coarse corners as in the Picard coefficient update. Velocities are transferred on the C grid, linearly along each component's own direction and with 3/4–1/4 weights across it, and restriction is the transpose of prolongation. The smoother is ℓ1-Jacobi, whose diagonal bounds the absolute row sum of the operator, so every sweep is convergent and the V-cycle is symmetric positive definite, as conjugate gradients require. Wall and ice-free velocity points are excluded from the transfers. The coarse coefficients are rebuilt at the start of every conjugate-gradient solve, so the preconditioner follows the coefficients through Picard iterations.

  • levels::Any: coarse levels, finest first, each a NamedTuple of grid, coefficients and work vectors

  • residual::Any: residual workspace on the finest grid

  • smoothing_sweeps::Int64: ℓ1-Jacobi sweeps before and after each coarse-grid correction

  • coarsest_sweeps::Int64: ℓ1-Jacobi sweeps on the coarsest grid

Nunatak.SubgridGroundedFraction — Type
struct SubgridGroundedFraction

Sub-grid grounded fraction from the flotation function f = H + ρw b / ρᵢ (sea level at z = 0), interpolated linearly between cell centers, so φ ∈ [0, 1] moves continuously as the grounding line crosses a cell. At a velocity point φᵘ, φᵛ is the grounded part of the segment joining the two neighboring centers; at a center φ is the grounded part of the cell. Friction is scaled by the face fractions φᵘ, φᵛ and melt by 1 - φ. The densities come from IceSheetConstants.

mutable struct VelocityConjugateGradientSolver{L, AL, C, P, W}

Preconditioned conjugate gradient solver for a symmetric positive-definite operator acting on a VelocityVector. The operator follows the Oceananigans convention linear_operation!(q, p, args...); adjoint_operation! defaults to linear_operation! because the Picard (SSA/DIVA) operator is self-adjoint.

  • adjoint_operation!::Any: adjoint operator adjoint_operation!(q, p, args...)

  • preconditioner::Any: preconditioner, nothing or JacobiPreconditioner

  • residual::Any: residual

  • preconditioned_residual::Any: preconditioned residual

  • search_direction::Any: search direction

  • operator_product::Any: operator applied to the search direction

  • dot_workspace::Any: workspace for elementwise products in dot products

  • iteration::Int64: iterations taken by the last solve

Nunatak.WeertmanFriction — Type
struct WeertmanFriction{C, FT}

Weertman sliding law τ_b = C |u_b|^(m-1) u_b, evaluated with the basal velocity u_b. The speed is regularized, |u_b|² + u₀², so the drag coefficient stays finite at rest.

  • coefficient::Any: friction coefficient C, a Field at (Center, Center, Nothing)

  • exponent::Any: sliding exponent m

  • velocity_regularization::Any: speed regularization u₀ (m s⁻¹)

Nunatak.checkpointed_gradient! — Function
checkpointed_gradient!(dmodel, cost, model, Δt, Nt; checkpoint_interval = ceil(Int, sqrt(Nt)))

Gradient of cost(model) after Nt time steps of size Δt with respect to the initial state and the parameter fields, accumulated into the shadow dmodel (from Enzyme.make_zero(model)), and the value of the cost. The forward run stores prognostic_state every checkpoint_interval steps; the reverse pass restores each checkpoint with restore_prognostic_state! and differentiates its segment, so only one segment is taped at a time. model is left at the final state. Requires Enzyme. test/test_enzyme.jl checks the gradient against finite differences.
